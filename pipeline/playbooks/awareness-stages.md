# Awareness stages

How much the viewer already knows decides what the piece must do. Every brief, concept and post carries one stage in `awareness_stage`. The stage comes from the anchor: the pain or desire, quoted in the audience's own words, tells you where they are. Results are compared by stage, so a piece without one cannot be judged fairly.

## Stages

| Stage (`awareness_stage`) | What the viewer believes | What the piece must do | Proof that works | Main metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unaware | Nothing is wrong, or the problem is just how things are | Name the problem in their words before naming anything else. No product in the first beat | A relatable moment, a surprising fact with a source | 3 s hold, completion |
| problem_aware | Something is wrong; they do not know it can be fixed | Make the cost of the problem concrete, then show a way out exists | The cost of doing nothing, a before state, a confession | completion, comments |
| solution_aware | Fixes exist; they do not know which one | Show how this way works and why it beats the usual way | A demo, a walkthrough, a comparison | saves, shares |
| product_aware | They know this brand or product; they are not sure yet | Answer the objection that stops them | Results, reviews, a receipt, a guarantee | link clicks, profile visits |
| most_aware | They want it; they need a reason to act now | Give the offer and the next step plainly | The offer itself, a deadline that is real | conversions, bookings |

## Rules

1. One stage per piece. A piece that tries to move two stages at once does neither.
2. Read the stage from the anchor, not from the objective. An `awareness` objective can still target a problem-aware viewer.
3. Unaware and problem-aware pieces never open with the brand or the product.
4. Most-aware pieces never invent urgency. A deadline that is not real is R-FACT.
5. Pair the stage with a visual format from `visual-formats.md` that suits it. A mismatch is allowed only with a one-line reason.
