# Creative rubric

A short-form explainer or ad is scored on these 10 criteria, 0 to 10 each, out of 100.
A piece scores 8 or more overall only with at least 8 on hook, viewer problem, pacing and motion, captions and sound, and at least 7 on the rest.
Score with evidence: the line, the timestamp, the measured number.

## Criteria

| # | Id (`rubric_id`) | Criterion | 8 or more looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | R-HOOK | Hook in the first 2 s | By 2.0 s the viewer has heard and read one specific, slightly surprising idea, and something has moved on screen. The hook is about the viewer or a tension, not a product slogan. Spoken, on-screen text and visual say the same thing in three ways. |
| 2 | R-PROBLEM | A stated viewer problem | Within 3 to 10 s, one sentence names the viewer's situation and its cost, in their words, before the product is explained. |
| 3 | R-TAKEAWAY | One takeaway per beat | Each beat leaves one sentence the viewer could repeat, not a label. The takeaway is the "so what" of the feature. |
| 4 | R-PROOF | Proof or demonstration | At least one moment where the viewer sees the thing work: a real screen, a built artefact, a concrete number, or a sourced claim shown with its source. |
| 5 | R-LOOP | Curiosity loop | An open question or count set up early and paid off late, so leaving early means missing the answer. Ideally one loop per 15 to 20 s. |
| 6 | R-TRADEOFF | Honest trade-off and credibility | One plainly named limitation of the product, placed where it builds trust. Facts concrete, sourced, and never repeated as filler. |
| 7 | R-PACING | Pacing and motion | A visual change every 1 to 3 s that is driven by the words: elements build on the spoken noun, the camera pushes, pans or follows a line between scenes. No dead frames. Cuts are motivated or replaced by camera moves. |
| 8 | R-CAPTIONS | Caption style | One caption layer, 1 to 4 words at a time, synced to the actual speech, one highlighted keyword, in a fixed safe position that never covers the presenter or the diagram. Labels on the diagram are separate and short. |
| 9 | R-SOUND | Sound | VO cut to picture; a music bed under the whole video (ducked under VO, up in the gaps); one sound effect family tied to the visual grammar (tick on each step, whoosh on camera moves, click on locks); no silence longer than 0.5 s unless it is a deliberate beat. |
| 10 | R-CTA | Call to action | One next step, spoken and shown once, specific about what the viewer gets, and visually connected to the payoff. |

## When it runs

1. Concepts: the creative director pre-scores each concept on criteria 1 to 6 and recommends the highest, not the closest copy of a reference.
2. Storyboard: before the price, a reviewer who did not write it scores the script and storyboard on all 10 (sound and captions from the plan). Any criterion under 7 goes back to the creative director with the fix named; it never reaches the price. The reviewer also checks variety: neighbouring panels differ in shot size, angle or place, and props or framing do not repeat across the set unless the story needs it.
3. Finished video: after finishing, score the file with measured inputs (hard cuts per minute, the longest silence, whether the captions follow the speech) and show the score to the person with the rough cut, next to the reference's score when there is a reference.

## Rules

1. A score without evidence does not count.
2. Measure what can be measured: cuts with a scene-change filter, silences with a silence detector, caption timing against the voice-over files.
3. Never raise a score because the person is in a hurry; say what would raise it instead.
